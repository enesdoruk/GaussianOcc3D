GaussianOcc3D: Adaptive Multi-modal Fusion for 3D Occupancy Prediction Using Gaussians

A. Enes Doruk1 · Hasan F. Ates1
1 Ozyegin University, Istanbul, Turkiye
ArXiv Code BibTeX

Overview of GaussianOcc3D. Camera and LiDAR features are lifted into a shared Gaussian space, refined through Gaussian extractors, adaptively fused, and decoded into the final 3D occupancy prediction.

Abstract

3D semantic occupancy prediction aims to provide a dense and fine-grained understanding of the surrounding environment. Multi-modal systems combine the semantic richness of cameras with the geometric precision of LiDAR, but adaptively aligning these fundamentally different representations remains challenging.

GaussianOcc3D addresses this issue by lifting camera and LiDAR features into a shared continuous 3D Gaussian representation. The framework combines LiDAR Depth Feature Aggregation (LDFA), Entropy-Based Feature Smoothing (EBFS), and Adaptive Camera-LiDAR Fusion (ACLF) to improve geometric grounding, reduce cross-modal inconsistency, and dynamically weight sensor contributions according to reliability.

Evaluations on SurroundOcc, Occ3D, and SemanticKITTI demonstrate strong performance across all three benchmarks, reaching 29.3, 49.8, and 25.8 mIoU, respectively.

Method

GaussianOcc3D uses 3D Gaussians as a unified representation for camera semantics and LiDAR geometry. The two modalities are lifted independently, refined through Gaussian extractors, smoothed before fusion, and finally combined through reliability-aware adaptive weighting.

Overview of the proposed GaussianOcc3D framework.

LiDAR Depth Feature Aggregation (LDFA).

Adaptive Camera-LiDAR Fusion (ACLF).

LDFA

LiDAR Depth Feature Aggregation

Aggregates sparse LiDAR geometry using chunk-based vertical refinement and cross-depth attention, preserving informative depth structures while reducing noise from empty regions.

EBFS

Entropy-Based Feature Smoothing

Quantifies camera–LiDAR discrepancy in overlapping Gaussian representations through bidirectional cross-entropy and smooths inconsistent descriptors before fusion.

ACLF

Adaptive Camera-LiDAR Fusion

Uses dual-stream cross-context, soft gating, and consistency-aware reweighting to dynamically select reliable camera and LiDAR evidence at the Gaussian level.
